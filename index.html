Walk-in tub with shower

A walk-in tub with shower: one fixture for the soaker and the stand-up showerer

Direct answer: a walk-in tub with shower is a walk-in tub (watertight door, built-in seat, low step) that also has a working shower: a hand shower on a slide bar, usually a fixed head up high, a valve that feeds both, and waterproof walls with a curtain or screen. It suits homes where one person wants a seated soak and someone else wants a quick standing shower. If nobody soaks, a walk-in shower is simpler.

How two bathing styles share one tub

The whole point of a combo is that nobody in the house has to give up the way they like to bathe. A typical day in a two-person home looks like this:

  1. Morning, the stand-up showerer. Steps over the low door ledge, latches the door, draws the curtain inside the rim and showers under the fixed head. No filling, no waiting. The textured floor and a grab bar at standing height do the work a tub wall used to do.
  2. Evening, the soaker. Sits on the seat, closes the door, fills the tub and soaks. The hand shower rinses hair without leaning back and keeps the shoulders warm while the tub fills.
  3. The drain. The door cannot open until the water is gone, so the soaker stays seated and runs the hand shower over their shoulders while the tub empties. How to make the drain wait easier.
  4. Hot water in between. A morning shower and an evening bath are easy on most heaters. A shower and a full bath in the same hour are not. Check your heater against both.

Questions people ask about walk-in tubs with showers

What is a walk-in tub with shower?

It is a walk-in tub, with a watertight door and a built-in seat, that also has a shower: a hand shower on a slide bar, often a fixed head up high, a valve that can feed both, and waterproof walls with a curtain or glass screen. One person can soak seated while another showers standing.

Can you take a shower in a walk-in tub without filling it?

Yes. With the door closed and latched you can shower standing on the textured floor or sitting on the seat with the hand shower, and the water simply runs down the drain. Keep the curtain or screen inside the rim and aim the spray away from the door seal.

Will a walk-in tub with shower fit in my bathtub space?

Usually. Many models are built for the common 60-inch alcove, and shorter models around 52 inches exist. Check the drain end, the narrowest doorway on the delivery path, and whether your walls are waterproof above the shower head.

How long do you wait for a walk-in tub to drain?

It depends on the tub, the drain design and your home's drain line, so check the spec sheet and ask the installer. On a combo you can run the hand shower over your shoulders while the tub empties, which keeps you warm and rinses you off at the same time.

Do I need a bigger water heater for a walk-in tub with shower?

Often. Published occupied capacities run from about 30 to 90 gallons, and roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of a warm bath comes from the heater. If someone also showers in the same hour, compare the total with your heater's first-hour rating. The water heater sizing page does the math.

Is a curtain or glass better on a walk-in tub with shower?

A curtain with a liner that hangs inside the tub rim suits most households, especially with a caregiver, because it slides fully out of the way. A fixed glass screen at the shower end looks neater for steady standing showerers. Full sliding enclosures are hard to fit around the tub door.

Is a walk-in tub with shower better than a roll-in shower?

Not for wheelchair users. A walk-in tub still has a door ledge and a seat to transfer onto, while a roll-in shower has no curb. The combo wins when someone in the home wants to soak and every bather can manage a low step.
