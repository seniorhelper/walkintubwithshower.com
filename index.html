Homeowners & aging in place

Bath days and shower days in the same space

A traditional bathtub often forces a choice: climb over a tall wall for every shower, or give up soaking altogether for a step-in shower. A thoughtful walk in tub with shower gives you both, with a low threshold, a seat and a shower you can enjoy standing or sitting.

On days when joints feel stiff, you can sit, close the door and let warm water and hydrotherapy surround you. On busy mornings when you just want to rinse and go, you can use the shower quickly without filling the tub.

  • Low-threshold entry designed for more predictable movement in and out.
  • Chair-height seating that may feel easier on hips and knees than a floor-level tub.
  • Option to shower standing or seated with a handheld or mounted shower wand.
  • Space-saving design that fits where most traditional tubs used to sit.

Emotional benefits that matter just as much as features

When you or someone you love starts to worry about bathing, it is not only about surfaces and grab bars. It is about dignity, privacy and feeling like the bathroom is still a place of calm instead of stress.

The right walk in tub with shower can help:

💡 Reduce dread Bath time can shift from "I hope nothing goes wrong" to "I have a routine that fits how I feel today."
🧡 Support independence With good planning, many older adults can continue bathing with less hands-on assistance.
🤝 Ease caregiver strain Transfers often become more predictable, which can help reduce stress for helpers as well.

No product can remove every risk, but design details and routines can help lower the chance of rushed, unstable movements.

The Real Difference Between a Walk In Tub and a Walk In Tub With Shower

A standard walk in tub is designed primarily for soaking. You enter the tub, sit down, close the watertight door, fill the tub, and then drain it before you can exit. While this is a significant improvement over a traditional tub for seniors with mobility limitations, it does have a well-known limitation: you must wait inside the tub while it fills and drains. On cold mornings, that wait can be uncomfortable.

A walk in tub with shower solves this by incorporating a full shower system — typically a handheld wand on a slide bar, and sometimes a fixed overhead head as well. Consequently, you get the flexibility of a quick morning shower on weekdays and a therapeutic soak with hydrotherapy jets on the evenings when your joints need warmth. Furthermore, the shower function makes the tub usable even when a full soak is not practical, which means you get significantly more value from a single installation.

Hydrotherapy: What It Is and Who Benefits Most

Hydrotherapy is one of the most common reasons seniors and aging-in-place homeowners consider a walk in tub. In the context of a walk in tub with shower, hydrotherapy typically refers to water jets (also called whirlpool jets) that direct pressurized warm water at specific areas of the body, and air jets that push thousands of micro-bubbles through the water for a gentler, full-body sensation.

Many of our clients report meaningful relief from arthritis symptoms, lower back stiffness, leg circulation issues, and general muscle tension after regular use. That said, we always recommend talking with your doctor before beginning a hydrotherapy routine, particularly if you have cardiovascular conditions, diabetes, or open wounds. We can provide product information you can bring to that conversation.

Who benefits most from a walk in tub shower combination

Seniors with arthritis, joint pain, or reduced balance. Homeowners planning ahead for aging in place. Adults recovering from surgery or injury. Caregivers managing daily bathing routines. Families who want one bathroom to work for multiple generations.

Features that matter most for daily comfort

A low door threshold (ideally under 3 inches) is the single most impactful safety feature. After that, seat height, heated seating for cold starts, a fast-drain system, and controls reachable from a seated position all significantly improve the daily experience.

    Frequently asked questions about walk in tub and shower combinations

    These are some of the questions seniors, caregivers and facilities ask us most often.
    Will a walk in tub with shower fit where my current bathtub is?›

    In many homes, yes. Most walk in tub with shower systems are designed to fit in a standard alcove where a typical bathtub sits now. However, every bathroom is different. During a consultation, we look at photos, measurements and doorways to see how realistic the fit will be.

    Can I still take quick showers without filling the tub?›

    Yes. One of the main reasons people choose a tub and shower combination is flexibility. You can use the shower alone for a quicker routine, or fill the tub for a full soak when you want warmth and hydrotherapy.

    How long does it take to fill and drain a walk in tub?›

    Times vary depending on your home's water pressure, plumbing and water heater size, as well as the specific tub model. During planning, we can talk about general ranges and ways to keep the experience as comfortable as possible.

    Do I have to use the jets every time?›

    No. A walk in tub with shower that includes hydrotherapy can be used as a simple soaker tub whenever you want. Jets can typically be turned off or set on very gentle settings depending on how you feel that day.

    Is hydrotherapy okay for my health conditions?›

    Only your doctor can answer this with confidence. We always encourage you to talk with your healthcare provider about warm water, soaking time and jet intensity. We can provide general product information that you can bring to your appointment.

    Will Medicare or insurance pay for a walk in tub and shower combination?›

    Many traditional Medicare plans and insurance carriers do not view walk in tubs as covered medical equipment. However, some Medicare Advantage plans, local programs or veterans' benefits may offer partial assistance for accessibility upgrades.

    Can a walk in tub with shower work in a very small bathroom?›

    Sometimes. There are compact models and front-entry designs that can help in tight spaces. The best first step is to measure your bathroom carefully and talk with us about door swings, clearances and any mobility devices you use.

    How do I clean and maintain the tub and shower?›

    Most modern units are designed with smooth surfaces and accessible components to help with routine cleaning. Some hydrotherapy systems include cleaning cycles or sanitation features. We review maintenance instructions before and after installation.

    What if my loved one has memory changes or dementia?›

    In these cases, simplicity is very important. We can talk about choosing a walk in tub shower combination with simpler controls, clear routines and support from caregivers so the experience feels predictable and calm.

    How long does installation usually take?›

    Many projects can be completed in one day in areas where one-day installation is available. More complex projects may take longer if plumbing or electrical changes are needed. We set realistic expectations so you are not left wondering what comes next.

    Can I use my own contractor instead of turnkey installation?›

    Yes. Aging Safely Baths can ship a walk in tub with shower directly to your home or contractor for DIY-style installation, as long as installation follows manufacturer guidelines. For some models, you may even qualify for a limited labor warranty when your own contractor installs the unit properly.

    Do walk in tub and shower combinations help property value?›

    Many buyers appreciate updated bathrooms and aging-in-place features. While no specific value increase can be guaranteed, thoughtful accessibility upgrades often make a home more attractive to a wider range of buyers.

    Can we try to reuse existing tile or finishes?›

    In some projects, part of the surrounding wall finish can stay. In others, replacing the surround makes more sense. We talk through goals for appearance and budget before finalizing any plan so you know what to expect.

    Does a walk in tub door swing in or out?›

    Both designs are made. Inward-swinging doors are the most common, and the water inside presses the door against its seal. Outward-swinging doors leave more room to step in or slide over from a wheelchair, but they need clear floor space in front of the tub. We check your bathroom layout before recommending one.

    Can I open the door while the tub is still full?›

    No. The door stays sealed until the water drains, because the weight of the water holds it closed and opening it would flood the floor. You stay seated while the tub drains, so a fast-drain option shortens the wait. Using the handheld shower to rinse while it drains helps you stay warm.

    Does a walk in tub need its own electrical circuit?›

    A soaking-only tub needs no electrical work. Tubs with air jets, whirlpool jets, a heated seat or an in-line heater usually need one or two dedicated GFCI-protected circuits. The manufacturer’s specification sheet lists the exact requirement, and a licensed electrician confirms your panel can handle it.
