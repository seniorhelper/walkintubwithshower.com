Handheld or fixed shower head on a walk-in tub?

Short answer: put a hand shower on every walk-in tub. It is the only kind you can use well while sitting on the seat, and it lets you rinse with warm water while the tub fills or drains. Add a fixed head as well if someone in the home showers standing up. For most combo households, the best setup is both: a fixed or adjustable head up high plus a hand shower on a slide bar, with a diverter to switch between them.

Why the hand shower matters so much on a walk-in tub

A walk-in tub has one quirk that a regular tub does not: you sit inside while the water comes in, and you sit inside while it goes out, because the door cannot open until the tub is empty. With a hand shower you can keep warm water running over your shoulders during both waits. Without one, you sit wet and cooling. The drain wait guide turns this into a simple routine.

It also makes the seated bath itself easier. You can rinse your hair without leaning back, wash your feet without bending far, and rinse soap off the seat and floor before you leave.

When a fixed head is worth adding

If someone in the home showers standing, they will want water coming from above without holding a wand. A fixed head, or a hand shower parked high on a slide bar, gives them that. On a combo, mount it so the spray falls toward the back wall and the seat, not straight at the door. Door seals are made to hold still water, not a steady spray.

What to look for in the hand shower

  • A long enough hose. The ADA standards ask for a hose at least 59 inches long on accessible bathtubs. That is a useful minimum for any walk-in tub: long enough to reach your feet from the seat and to hand to a helper.
  • A slide bar, not just a fixed holder. A bar lets a tall person park the head high and a seated person park it low. Some slide bars are built strong enough to double as a grab bar; most are not, so check before anyone leans on one.
  • A holder you can reach from the seat. Hanging the wand back up should not require standing.
  • An on/off or pause button on the handle. Handy for soaping up without the spray going everywhere.
  • An easy diverter. If you have both heads, the switch between them should be a large lever, not a small knob that needs a pinch grip.

The valve behind the shower

Whatever heads you choose, the valve that mixes the water matters for safety. Plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code require shower and tub-shower valves to be pressure-balanced, thermostatic, or a combination of the two, set to limit how hot the water can get. That protects against sudden temperature swings when someone flushes a toilet elsewhere in the house. Ask your installer which type the tub uses and what maximum temperature it is set to.

Also ask where the controls sit. On a combo, the tub filler and shower controls should be reachable from the seat without leaning over the water, and the shower control should be reachable standing up as well.

Questions people ask

Do walk-in tubs come with a shower head?

Many come with a hand shower on the deck or wall, and many can be ordered with a fuller shower setup. It depends on the model and brand, so ask exactly what is included in the quote.

Can I add a hand shower to a walk-in tub later?

Often yes, through the tub’s own faucet or a wall valve. It is easier and neater to plan it at the start, especially if you also want a fixed head and a diverter.

Can I use the fixed shower while sitting on the seat?

You can, but the water lands on your head and toward the door, and you cannot aim it. A hand shower is far better for seated bathing.

Will the shower spray leak through the walk-in tub door?

The door is sealed against water pressing on it from inside a full tub. A strong spray aimed straight at the seal can still find its way out. Aim fixed heads toward the back wall and keep a curtain or screen in place for standing showers.

How long should the hand shower hose be?

At least 59 inches, the minimum the ADA standards use for accessible bathtubs. Longer is fine if it does not drag on the floor or tangle around the seat.
