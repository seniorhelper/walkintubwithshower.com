Curtain, glass screen and splash control for a walk-in tub with shower

Short answer: a walk-in tub door is sealed to hold still water in a full tub. It is not designed to take a shower spray pointed straight at it. To keep the floor dry, do three things: give the shower its own waterproof walls, aim the spray away from the door, and hang a curtain or fixed glass screen whose bottom edge sits inside the tub rim. A curtain is cheaper and easier for helpers; glass looks neater but leaves less room to reach in.

Where water escapes on a walk-in tub shower

  1. The wall joint. Where the tub flange meets the wall surround or tile, a gap or failed caulk lets water run behind the tub. You often do not see this one until there is damage below.
  2. The door seal. The gasket is pressed shut by water inside a full tub. A strong spray hitting it from inside, while the tub is empty and the door is just latched, can push water past it.
  3. Over the rim. A curtain hanging outside the tub channels water down its face and onto the floor. A curtain hanging inside sends it back into the tub.

Getting the most from a curtain

  • Use a curved rod. It pushes the curtain out over the floor at the top and gives the standing showerer elbow room, while the liner still drops inside the rim.
  • Pick a weighted or magnetic-hem liner, so it does not blow in against your legs or out over the rim.
  • Choose a length that reaches just inside the tub rim. Too short and spray escapes below it; too long and it pools on the tub floor and stays wet.
  • Hang the rod high enough that the tallest person does not touch it, and never use it as a handhold. A tension rod or curtain rod is not a grab bar.

Getting the most from glass

  • A fixed screen at the shower end, with the door end left open, is the usual walk-in tub layout. At least one major walk-in tub maker sells a frameless bath screen as part of its shower package.
  • Make sure the screen clears the door swing and does not block the grab bar or the faucet.
  • Choose safety glass and ask how it is anchored. The tub flange and wall surround must be able to carry it.
  • Glass shows every water spot. A squeegee hung within reach of the seat makes daily wiping realistic.

Waterproofing the walls for the shower part

This is the step people skip. A soak-only walk-in tub splashes a little at the faucet. A shower soaks all three walls up to head height and above, every single day. Before you add a shower, the walls need a surface made for it:

Wall surround panels

Large acrylic, solid-surface or cultured-marble panels that overlap the tub flange. Few or no grout lines. Often the quickest choice on a one-day install.

Tile over a waterproof backer

Looks custom. Needs cement board or foam board with a waterproof membrane behind it, not just painted drywall, and grout that is kept sealed.

Existing tile, if it is sound

Fine if it runs above the shower head on all three walls and nothing is loose, cracked or soft behind it. Press on the lower tiles: any give is a warning sign.

Whatever the surface, the tub flange should sit behind it so water runs down the wall into the tub, and the joint should be sealed with a flexible, mildew-resistant sealant. Check that seal once or twice a year and redo it when it cracks.

Splash habits that help

  • Point the fixed head at the back wall, not the door.
  • When showering standing, close the curtain or screen before turning on the water.
  • Use the hand shower low and close to your body when seated.
  • Keep a slip-resistant mat with a rubber back outside the door, and wipe up drips before walking on them. The safety guide covers grab bars and footing for the standing showerer.
  • If water shows up on the floor after every shower, have the door seal and door alignment checked. A worn or out-of-line seal is a service item, not something to live with.

Questions people ask

Does a walk-in tub with shower need a shower curtain?

It needs something to keep the spray in: a curtain with a liner that hangs inside the tub rim, or a fixed glass screen. Without either, water from a standing shower ends up on the floor.

Can you put glass doors on a walk-in tub?

A fixed glass screen at one end is common and works well. A full sliding enclosure is harder, because it has to work around the tub door and still leave room to get in and sit. Ask the installer to check the door swing and grab bars before ordering glass.

Why is there water on the floor after I shower in my walk-in tub?

The usual causes are spray hitting the door seal, a curtain hanging outside the rim, or a worn or misaligned door gasket. Aim the shower at the back wall and tuck the liner inside first. If the leak continues, have the door seal checked.

Do I need new walls to add a shower to a walk-in tub?

Only if your current walls are not waterproof above the shower head on all three sides. Sound tile that goes high enough can stay. Painted drywall or half-height tile needs a wall surround or new tile.

Is a curtain or glass better for a caregiver?

A curtain. It slides fully out of the way, so a helper can reach the bather and the hand shower from the open side.
