Walk-in tub shower combo vs walk-in shower vs tub-to-shower conversion

Short answer: choose the combo if someone in the home truly wants to soak and someone else wants a stand-up shower. Choose a walk-in shower (which in most homes means a tub-to-shower conversion in the old tub alcove) if nobody soaks, if any step is too much, or if the soaker cannot sit through the fill and drain. If a wheelchair needs to roll in, go straight to a roll-in shower.

These three get mixed up because they all replace the same thing: the old bathtub with a tall wall. A quick definition of each:

Walk-in tub with shower

A deep tub with a watertight door and a built-in seat, plus a shower setup and waterproof walls. You can soak or shower.

Walk-in shower

A shower with a low curb or no curb, usually with a seat, grab bars and a hand shower. No tub at all.

Tub-to-shower conversion

The project that removes your old tub and puts a walk-in shower base in the same alcove. It is how most homes get a walk-in shower.

Choose the combo when

  • One person wants warm, seated soaks and another prefers a stand-up shower.
  • It is the only tub in the house and you want to keep one for family, grandchildren or a future buyer.
  • Every bather can manage a low step with a grab bar.
  • The soaker is comfortable sitting in the tub while it fills and drains, or will use a hand shower to stay warm.
  • Your water heater can handle a deep fill, or you are willing to upgrade it. The hot water check takes a minute.

Choose a walk-in shower or conversion when

  • Nobody in the home soaks, or would soak only rarely.
  • Any step at all is a struggle, or a curbless entry is safer.
  • The bather gets cold, anxious or impatient waiting in a tub.
  • You want the simplest daily routine and the least to maintain: no door seal, no fill, no drain wait.
  • There is another tub elsewhere in the home.

For kit sizes, drains and what a conversion involves, our sister site has a detailed tub-to-shower conversion guide.

Questions that settle it quickly

  1. Who soaks, and how often? If the honest answer is “nobody”, you have your answer.
  2. Who showers standing? If nobody does, a soak-only walk-in tub may be enough.
  3. Can everyone step over a few inches? If not, a shower with a lower entry wins.
  4. Is this the last tub in the house? Keeping one can matter more than people expect.
  5. What does the room allow? Check with the 60-inch alcove fit checker.

What about cost?

Prices depend heavily on the model, the walls, the plumbing and where you live, so we do not post one-size numbers here. What we can say: a combo adds the cost of the shower fixtures and waterproof walls on top of the tub itself, and any electrical work for jets or heaters. A conversion’s cost depends mostly on the shower base, the walls and how much floor and drain work is needed. Get written quotes for each option, with the same wall and fixture choices, so you compare like with like. We will gladly give you one for both.

Questions people ask

Is a walk-in tub with shower better than a walk-in shower?

Only if someone actually soaks. A combo gives you both a seated soak and a stand-up shower; a walk-in shower gives a lower entry, no waiting and a simpler routine. Pick based on how the people in your home bathe.

Is a tub-to-shower conversion the same as a walk-in shower?

Close. A walk-in shower is the result; a tub-to-shower conversion is the project that puts one where your old tub was. Most homes get a walk-in shower that way.

Can I convert a walk-in tub to a shower later?

Yes, it is possible, but it is a second full project: the tub comes out and a shower base goes in. If you are unsure, it is better to decide carefully the first time.

Which is easier for a caregiver?

For most caregivers, a walk-in shower with a curtain and a hand shower, because there is no door to work around and no fill or drain wait. A combo can still work well when the bather values soaking.

Which fits a small bathroom better?

Both usually fit a standard 60-inch alcove. A walk-in shower can also be built in shorter or square spaces, and shorter walk-in tubs exist too. Measure first, then compare models that fit.
