Combo, walk-in tub or walk-in shower? Answer 8 questions.

Short answer: a walk-in tub with a shower makes the most sense when the home has both kinds of bathers: someone who wants to sit and soak, and someone who would rather shower standing. If nobody soaks, a low-threshold walk-in shower is usually simpler. If a wheelchair has to roll in, neither fits and a roll-in shower is the right tool.

Pick one answer per question. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you choose to send it. You get a plain suggestion, the reasons behind it, and what to check before you buy.

1. How many people bathe in this bathroom?
2. Does anyone need to stay seated in a wheelchair or rolling shower chair to bathe?
3. Can every bather step over a low ledge of a few inches while holding a grab bar?

Walk-in tub doors still have a small step at the bottom, often a few inches tall.

4. How often does anyone want a warm, seated soak?
5. Does anyone prefer to shower standing up?
6. Is the person who soaks comfortable waiting inside while the tub fills and drains?

A walk-in tub door cannot open until the water is gone, so the bather sits through the fill and the drain.

7. Is this the only bathtub in the home?
8. What kind of water heater do you have?

What the four results mean

Walk-in tub with shower

A walk-in tub with a door and seat, plus a shower valve, hand shower or fixed head, and waterproof walls or a screen. Soak sitting down on bad days, rinse standing up on busy ones.

Soak-only walk-in tub

The same tub without a full shower setup. Often a hand shower on the deck for rinsing. A good fit when one person bathes and always sits.

Walk-in shower or tub-to-shower conversion

The old tub comes out and a low-threshold shower base goes in, usually with a seat and a hand shower. No soaking, no waiting for water.

Roll-in shower

A shower floor with no curb, sized so a wheelchair or rolling shower chair can go straight in. The comparison page sets it beside a combo and a walk-in shower.

Questions people ask

Is this helper a medical recommendation?

No. It sorts bathing habits and house facts. If a doctor or occupational therapist has told you how someone should bathe, follow that advice first and use the helper only to compare products.

Why does a wheelchair answer rule out a walk-in tub with shower?

Because every walk-in tub has a door ledge and a seat you have to move onto. Some people transfer from a chair to a tub seat well, but if the person must stay in the chair to bathe, a roll-in shower is the fixture built for that.

Can a walk-in tub with shower work for a couple with different habits?

Yes. That is the most common reason to choose one. The person who soaks gets the seat and the sealed door. The person who showers stands inside the tub and uses the shower with a curtain or screen in place.

What if two of the results score about the same?

Then the room usually decides. Measure the alcove with the fit checker, check your water heater, and call us with both options in mind.
