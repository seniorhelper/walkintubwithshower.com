Plumbing and hot water for a walk-in tub with shower

Short answer: in most homes a walk-in tub with shower connects to the same drain and water lines the old tub used. The things that change the job are: which end the drain is on, adding or replacing a shower valve in the wall, a dedicated electrical circuit if the tub has jets or a heater, and having enough hot water. Walk-in tubs hold more water than a regular tub, so the water heater is the most common surprise.

Free check: is your water heater big enough?

Printed on the label on the side of the tank.

How it works: a comfortable bath is roughly two parts hot water to one part cold, and a common rule of thumb is a tank of at least two-thirds of the tub’s capacity. This is an estimate, not a sizing calculation; a plumber confirms the real numbers for your home.

Hot water in plain numbers

A standard bathtub holds roughly 25 to 45 gallons. Walk-in tubs are deeper and commonly hold 40 to 80 gallons, with some larger models holding more. Because you sit inside while the tub fills, you also want it to fill quickly and stay warm.

  • Tank heaters: a common rule of thumb is a tank of at least two-thirds of the tub’s capacity. A 75-gallon tub points to a 50-gallon tank or larger.
  • Tankless heaters: they never “run out”, but they limit how many gallons per minute of hot water you get. If the tub filler asks for more than the heater can deliver at your winter water temperature, the tub fills slowly or runs cool.
  • Back-to-back use: a morning shower followed by a full bath draws on the same tank. Leave time for it to recover, or size up.
  • Options if you are short: a larger tank, a mixing valve on the heater, a model with smaller capacity, or an in-line heater that keeps the bath warm once full (it maintains temperature; it does not heat the fill).

Plumbing questions to ask before you sign

  1. Is the tub ordered with the drain on the same end as my current drain?
  2. Is the shower valve new, and is it pressure-balanced or thermostatic? What maximum temperature is it set to?
  3. Where will the controls be, and can I reach them from the seat?
  4. Does this tub need its own electrical circuit? Is that included in the quote?
  5. What happens to the price if the floor or drain line is in poor shape once the old tub is out?
  6. Who pulls the permit, if one is needed?

Questions people ask

Does a walk-in tub with shower need special plumbing?

Usually not special, just complete. It connects to the existing drain and supply lines. The extra pieces are the shower valve and heads, and possibly an electrical circuit for jets or a heater. Moving the drain or adding a wall valve makes the job bigger.

What size water heater do I need for a walk-in tub?

A common rule of thumb is a tank at least two-thirds of the tub’s capacity. For a 60-gallon tub, that is about a 40-gallon tank or larger; for a 75-gallon tub, about 50 gallons or larger. Your plumber confirms it for your home.

Will a tankless water heater work with a walk-in tub?

It can, if its flow rating at your winter water temperature keeps up with the tub filler. If not, the tub fills slowly or runs cool. Ask the installer to compare the two numbers.

Can the shower and tub share one valve?

Yes. Many combos use one valve with a diverter that sends water to the tub filler, the hand shower or a fixed head. Make sure the diverter is easy to operate with a weak grip.

Does an in-line heater fill the tub with hot water?

No. An in-line heater keeps water that is already in the tub warm while you soak. The hot water for the fill still comes from your water heater.
