Will a walk-in tub with shower fit your 60-inch alcove?

Short answer: usually, yes. Most walk-in tubs are built for the common 60-inch bathtub alcove, in widths of about 30 or 32 inches. Shorter models exist too. Length is rarely the problem. The usual troublemakers are the drain being on the wrong end, a narrow bathroom door on the delivery path, and walls that are not waterproof high enough for a shower.

You need a tape measure and about ten minutes. Measure in inches. If a wall is out of square, use the smallest number you get.

What each check means

Length (A)

Walk-in tubs for alcoves are made a little under the alcove size so they can slide in. A tub listed at about 59½ to 60 inches is meant for a 60-inch opening. If your alcove is a few inches longer than the tub, ask whether the model has an extension or filler panel. If it is shorter than the tub, that model will not go in without moving a wall.

Depth (B)

Depth is how far the tub comes out from the back wall. A 32-inch tub in a 30-inch alcove sticks out two inches past the side walls. That is often fine, but the curtain rod or glass screen line moves out with it, and the side walls need trim where the tub overhangs.

Drain end

Standing outside the tub, the drain is either on your left or your right. Walk-in tubs are sold as left-drain or right-drain models. Matching your existing drain keeps the job simple. Switching ends means opening the floor to move the drain line, which adds time and cost.

Delivery path

The tub has to get from the truck to the bathroom. It is usually carried on its side, so the smaller of its width and height has to clear the narrowest door on the way. Bathroom doors in older homes are often the tightest spot. Taking a door off its hinges buys a little room; if that is not enough, a different model may be the answer.

Clear floor in front (C)

You need room to stand, turn and sit at the door, and a helper may need room too. Outward-swinging doors also need their full width of open floor to swing. As a yardstick, the U.S. ADA standards use a 30 × 48-inch rectangle as the basic clear floor space for one person.

Shower walls

A soak-only tub can live with a short tile skirt. The moment you add a shower, the walls around the tub need a waterproof surface that runs above the shower head on all three sides. If yours stop partway up, plan on a wall surround kit or new tile. More on that in the curtain, glass and splash guide.

Questions people ask

Will a walk-in tub with shower fit where my old bathtub is?

In most homes with a standard 60-inch tub alcove, yes. Check three things besides length: which end the drain is on, the narrowest doorway on the way in, and whether the walls are waterproof high enough for a shower.

How do I measure an alcove that is not square?

Measure the length along the back wall at the floor, at about 30 inches up and near the top of where the tub will sit. Use the smallest number. Do the same for depth on both side walls.

Is a 32-inch-wide tub too big for a 30-inch alcove?

Not always. It will stick out about two inches past the side walls. That works in many bathrooms, but it moves your curtain or screen line and needs trim at the overhang. Check that the front clearance still leaves room to sit at the door.

Does the tub height matter for fitting?

For the room itself, rarely. For delivery, yes: the tub is usually carried on its side, so its height or width, whichever is smaller, has to clear the narrowest doorway.

What if the checker says it will not fit?

It may still be possible with a different model, a door taken off its hinges, or a small wall change. Send us your numbers and photos and we will tell you honestly what it would take.
