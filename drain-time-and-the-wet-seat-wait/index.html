Drain time and the wet-seat wait in a walk-in tub with shower

Direct answer: you cannot open a walk-in tub door until the water is gone, so every soak ends with you seated, wet, while the tub empties. How long that takes depends on the water in the tub, the drain design and your home’s drain line, so get the figure for your model from the spec sheet and your installer. On a combo, the shower changes the wait: run the hand shower over your shoulders while the tub drains, and the wait becomes the rinse you would take anyway.

This is the complaint people raise most about walk-in tubs, and it is a fair one. It is also the place where a tub with a shower has a real edge over a soak-only tub, as long as the shower is set up for it.

Why the door stays shut

A walk-in tub door sits in the side wall of the tub, below the water line. On the common inward-swinging design, the water in the tub pushes the door outward against its gasket, which is what keeps the seal tight. Unlatching it with water inside would flood the floor, and a flooded floor is where falls happen. So the order never changes: sit, close the door, fill, soak, drain, open.

The standing showerer in the house never meets this problem. A shower with the door closed drains as it goes, and the door opens as soon as the water is off. The wait belongs only to the soaker.

What sets the drain time

FactorWhat it means for you
Water in the tubMore water takes longer. Spec sheets list occupied capacities from about 30 to 90 gallons across the models we track. Filling only to the level you need shortens both the fill and the drain.
Drain designSome tubs have more than one drain. Ella’s spec sheets, for example, show two independent 2-inch floor drains on the Royal. Ask whether the model has a single, dual or pump-assisted drain.
Your home’s drain lineA tub drains no faster than the pipe under the floor allows. An old, narrow or partly blocked line slows even a fast-drain tub. Spec-sheet times assume good plumbing.
Hair and soapA partly clogged stopper adds minutes. Clean it on a schedule.

For typical fill and drain ranges, our sister site has a walk-in tub fill and drain time calculator. If you already have a walk-in tub, the simplest number of all is the one you measure: time a full drain once with a kitchen timer.

A drain-wait routine that keeps you warm

  1. Warm the room first. The National Institute on Aging advises keeping the home at 68°F or warmer, because even mildly cool rooms can chill older adults. A cold bathroom makes the drain feel twice as long.
  2. Put a towel where you can reach it seated. A hook or bar beside the seat, not across the room.
  3. Use the hand shower during the fill. Warm water on your shoulders while the tub rises takes the edge off the start of the bath, too.
  4. Open the drain, then pick up the hand shower. Rinse your hair and shoulders while the level drops. This is the rinse you would take after a bath anyway; on a combo it happens during the wait.
  5. Towel your upper body before you stand. The NIA also advises getting out of damp clothing quickly in the cold. Drying off seated means less time standing wet.
  6. Stand only when the tub is empty, using the grab bar, then open the door and step out onto a rubber-backed mat.

If you are still shopping, two options help. A heated seat and backrest is listed as an optional feature on Ella models, and a hand shower within reach of the seat on a long hose matters more than any jet package. Ella’s five-piece faucet pairs a 1.9-gallon-per-minute hand shower with a 5-foot hose. The shower head guide covers hose length and holders.

When the wait is a deal-breaker

Some people find any wait seated in a cooling tub stressful, and no routine fixes that. If that describes the person who would soak, a soak is probably not worth it, and a low-threshold walk-in shower with a seat and hand shower gives the same seated wash with no wait at all. The comparison page lays the options side by side, and the decision helper asks about the wait directly.

Never open the door to hurry the drain. If you feel unwell in the tub, open the drain, call for help, and stay seated until the water is down. Keep a phone or a personal alert button within reach during every bath.

Questions people ask

Why can't you get out of a walk-in tub until it drains?

The door is in the side of the tub, below the water line. While there is water inside, it presses the door against its seal, and opening it would send the water across the bathroom floor. You stay seated until the tub is empty.

How do you stay warm while a walk-in tub drains?

Warm the bathroom before the bath, keep a towel where you can reach it from the seat, and run the hand shower over your shoulders while the water goes down. A heated seat and backrest, offered as an option on some models, helps too.

Does a walk-in tub with shower drain faster than one without?

Not because of the shower. Drain time depends on how much water is in the tub, the tub's drain design and your home's drain line. What the shower changes is the wait itself: you can rinse off under warm water instead of sitting wet and cooling.

How much hot water does rinsing during the drain use?

A hand shower limited to 1.9 gallons per minute, run for 5 minutes, uses about 9.5 gallons, of which roughly 7 to 8 come from the water heater with typical temperatures. Count it when you size the heater.
